# Two Pizzas: data-protection review (GDPR, ePrivacy, Romanian practice), 18 November 2026

Scope: privacy text `privacy.p1`-`p6` (EN, RO), what the code processes, infrastructure as described, device storage,
legal bases, children, controller duties, firm identity. Not legal advice; items marked **[lawyer]** need a lawyer's or
the authority's answer. Nothing in the repository was changed.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The design is privacy-friendly: no accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no third-party origin (fonts self-hosted, CSP
`connect-src` limited to the game's own host), names optional, notes capped at 140 characters, rooms deleted
automatically, logs carry no names/notes/codes/addresses, tokens held only as hashes, the public list holds no
personal data. The code is better than the text. The **text is not yet sufficient as the art. 13 information**: it lacks
the controller's identity and contact, the legal bases, the recipients (processors) and transfers, the rights and the
right to complain. Two statements are incomplete in a way that can mislead (IP address "not kept"; "deleted").
Readiness today: **5/10**; **8/10** once the Required items and the off-page checklist are done.

## Findings

### Required by law

**R1. Controller identity and contact are not on the page (GDPR art. 13(1)(a), art. 12(1)).**
A data subject must be told who the controller is and how to reach them; without a contact point the rights in
art. 15-21 cannot be exercised and art. 12(2) ("facilitate the exercise") is not met. The owner's removal of the identity
block from the page conflicts with this *unless the identity sits in the privacy text*. Minimum: legal name (FAIGO SRL),
registered office, one contact e-mail address (a postal address alone is lawful but impractical). Where: first lines of
the folded privacy text, one line. No separate page and no extra visible element is needed; one contact address is
unavoidable. The CUI/CIF and trade-register number are not required by the GDPR (see R7).
Fix: first paragraph of the proposed text below. **[lawyer]** only to confirm the wording of the company details.

**R2. Purposes are partly stated, legal bases not at all (art. 13(1)(c)).**
Recommended bases (the controller decides; a lawyer should confirm):
- Running the game for the people who join (names, notes, votes, actions): **art. 6(1)(b)**, the player asks for the
  service and the data is necessary to deliver it (EDPB Guidelines 2/2019: a free service offered on stated terms can be
  a contract; the narrower reading of "necessary" is satisfied because the game cannot work without these data). Alternative:
  6(1)(f). Do not use consent (it would force a withdrawal mechanism and a banner-like flow for no gain).
- IP address for rate limiting and abuse prevention: **art. 6(1)(f)** legitimate interest (security of the service, recital 49). Cost to the
  person is very low (transient, not stored by the code, no profile). Write a three-line balancing test (see checklist).
  Because 6(1)(f) is used, the text must say that the person can **object** (art. 21; art. 13(2)(b); art. 21(4) requires
  this right to be brought clearly to attention).
- Public result: contains no personal data in the controller's hands (generated team name, counts, score, date; nothing
  links it to a person; the room that held the only link is deleted). Then no legal basis is needed, because the GDPR does not apply. If someone
  argued the generated name plus date is identifying, 6(1)(f) covers it. The facilitator's button is an opt-in anyway, but it is not
  "consent" in the GDPR sense and the text should not call it that. **[lawyer]** to confirm the "not personal data" view (recital 26).
Fix: one clause per purpose in the text (proposed below).

**R3. Recipients, processors and transfers are not mentioned (art. 13(1)(e),(f)); the agreements must exist (art. 28).**
- Cloudflare (network proxy, Worker, Durable Objects, rate limiter) is a **processor** for the room data and request data.
  Its customer DPA is part of the online terms and includes the EU standard contractual clauses; Cloudflare is also
  certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (verify the current listing; I could not check the accounts).
  For some security and network-analytics data Cloudflare may act as an independent controller under its own policy: say
  "providers" generically and let the lawyer confirm.
- The static host is a **processor** for the access logs of the page. A DPA with it must exist (most hosts include one in their
  terms; check, and check where the host is located and how long it keeps access logs).
- Other players in the room are recipients of the name and notes (already partly said in p5; say it for names too).
- International transfers: Cloudflare operates globally; the Worker runs at the edge location nearest the user, and a Durable
  Object, by default, is created near its first request and **may be placed anywhere**. Cloudflare's public data-location
  documentation (checked 18 November 2026) states that a Durable Object can be restricted to a **jurisdiction** (`"eu"`
  or `"fedramp"`) so it "only runs and stores data within a region to comply with local regulations such as the GDPR", that
  Workers can still reach such objects from anywhere, and that **the Durable Object id is logged outside the jurisdiction for
  billing and debugging**. The code does not use it (`env.ROOM.getByName(code)`; `LEADERBOARD.getByName(...)`). Using
  `env.ROOM.jurisdiction('eu').getByName(code)` would keep rooms' storage and execution in the EU. This is **strongly recommended**
  (below) rather than Required, because transfers under SCCs/DPF are lawful, but the text must still say that some processing can
  happen outside the EEA if the restriction is not used or for edge delivery.
Fix: paragraph on providers and transfers (proposed text); sign/accept the DPAs (checklist).

**R4. Rights and the right to complain are missing (art. 13(2)(b),(d); art. 15-21).**
Say: access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection (and portability does not apply to processing under 6(1)(f); under 6(1)(b)
it applies in theory, but there is nothing automated to port); the right to complain to ANSPDCP (www.dataprotection.ro, Bd. General Gheorghe Magheru 28-30, sector 1, Bucharest;
verify the address before publishing; plain text, not a link, to respect the three-links rule). Also say whether giving data is
required (13(2)(e)): nothing is required; the name is optional. State that there is no automated decision-making
(13(2)(f) needs a statement only if there is one, so a one-liner is optional).
Practical point for the text: with no accounts, the firm cannot identify a requester unless they give the room code and
the name they used, and by then the room is usually deleted (art. 11(2): the firm is not obliged to collect more data to identify
people). A single sentence handles this.

**R5. The IP sentence can mislead (art. 5(1)(a) transparency; art. 13).**
"Your IP address is used only to limit how often rooms can be created or joined, and the game does not keep it." The *game code*
does not keep it, but the static host and Cloudflare do process it (every page request, every WebSocket upgrade, security
filtering). A reader will take it as "no one keeps my IP". Fix: say the providers handle it in ordinary technical logs
to deliver the page and keep the service safe (proposed text).

**R6. "Deleted" must be literally true, or worded precisely (art. 5(1)(a),(e), art. 13(2)(a)).**
Cloudflare's SQLite-backed Durable Objects offer **point-in-time recovery for up to 30 days** (Cloudflare's SQLite storage API page,
checked 18 November 2026). The code calls `deleteAll()` when the room ends, which is the correct application-level
erasure, but I could not establish from public documentation whether recoverable history of a deleted object remains
accessible to the platform for any time. Action for the owner: ask Cloudflare (support or DPA sub-processor documentation) or read the PITR
documentation to confirm that a deleted object's data is unrecoverable by anyone, or accept a short platform-side retention and say "deleted from
the game's server; the provider's own backups may keep a copy for up to 30 days" (a minimal and honest wording, to be used only if confirmed).
Also: the all-time public list is retained for as long as a row is in a top 10; the text must say so (it holds no personal data, so this is
informational). And update `emptyGraceMs` from 5 to 15 minutes in `core/config.ts` and the spec section 5 (the text already follows the config).

**R7. Identity under Romanian e-commerce law (Law 365/2002, art. 5) [lawyer].**
Law 365/2002 transposes Directive 2000/31/EC. Art. 5 obliges *providers of information-society services* to make permanently and easily available:
name, registered office, e-mail and other contact data, trade-register number, CUI/CIF (and VAT number, authorisation if any). The question is
whether a free game is an "information-society service". The Directive's recital 18 says the definition covers services
that are not paid by the recipient when they form part of an economic activity (advertising-funded, or promoting the provider's business). A free game published by a company
under its own brand to build its reputation fits that reading, so the conservative position is: **yes, give the identification**. Minimum:
name, registered office, CIF, trade-register number, contact e-mail; the person with the right to be told does not need the share capital.
It can sit in the same folded paragraph as R1 (one line), or the owner can decide to accept the (small) risk that the authority for
consumer protection treats a free, non-commercial game as outside the scope. The owner's decision to keep the page free of the firm's identity is
compatible with either only if the identity is in the privacy text. I would not rely on the "free service" argument.

### Strongly recommended

**S1. Put Durable Objects in the EU jurisdiction.** `env.ROOM.jurisdiction('eu')` and the same for `LEADERBOARD`.
Caveats from the documentation: the object id is still logged outside the region; Workers (the edge code that forwards) still run near the user and
requests may be handled by Cloudflare data centres outside the EU (full control needs Regional Services, an Enterprise feature; not realistic). Changing it
affects only new objects; do it **before** publication. It changes `worker/src/index.ts`, so it needs a test of the stubs. It also lets the text say "room data is stored in the EU".

**S2. Prompt near the name and note fields.** Placeholder or hint text such as "No real names or private details about others" (RO: "Fără nume reale sau
detalii private despre alții"). Free text in a work setting can contain third-party personal data; the controller cannot send art. 14 notices to people it does not
know, and art. 14(5)(b) (disproportionate effort) plus the very short retention carry that, but a hint is the cheapest way to show art. 25 (privacy by design). Do not make it a banner.

**S3. Written legitimate-interest assessment for the IP/rate-limit use** (purpose: keep a free service available against floods and room guessing; necessity: no
less intrusive way, because a limiter needs a per-client key; balance: no profile, not stored by the game, short-lived counters, expected by users). Half a page, in the firm's records.

**S4. Check what the two providers log and keep.** Static host: retention of access logs (the usual is 30-90 days; ask for it or reduce it). Cloudflare: retention of
request logs for the zone (they keep short-lived logs for security) and that no Logpush or Workers log target is enabled on the zone. The Worker's own logs are off in `wrangler.toml`;
verify in the dashboard that "Workers Logs" and "Traces" are indeed off in production (the setting in the repository can be overridden in the dashboard). Do not run `wrangler tail` in production during games
(the project notes already say this).

**S5. Verify that the platform's rate limiter does not persist the address.** Cloudflare's rate-limit binding is documented as local and approximate counters;
the sentence "the game does not keep it" is then about the game. Confirm in documentation or with support; I did not fetch that page.

**S6. Keep the privacy text and the code in step by test.** A test that fails when `emptyGraceMs`, `lifetimeMs` or the stored keys change without the text
changing would stop the drift that the handover shows has happened three times (item 8 of the security evaluation).

### Good practice

- **Children.** No consent is relied on, so art. 8 GDPR (age of digital consent; Romania: 16, Law 190/2018 art. 8) is not triggered, and the service is not aimed at children. Nothing is required.
  Art. 12(1) asks for intelligible information "in particular for children" only where the service addresses them. One optional sentence ("made for adults at work") is enough; if a minor
  plays, data minimisation and the 2-hour deletion already protect them. Do not add age gates (they would force more data).
- **DPIA (art. 35): not required.** No large-scale special-category data, no systematic monitoring, no profiling, no vulnerable group targeted. Write one line in the records saying why.
- **DPO (art. 37): not required** (not a public body; no large-scale monitoring or special-category processing as core activity).
- **Wording.** Keep "browser storage" language plain; no mention of "cookies" beyond "no cookie" (correct: none are set).
- **Local-only tour.** Say in one sentence that the solo tour sends nothing (it is true per spec section 6).
- **Open-source repository.** Public repo contains no personal data; keep it so (the project rules already forbid names, e-mails and hosts).

## Storage on the device: is a banner needed?

**No banner is needed for either item**, in my assessment (confirm with the lawyer).

- Rule: Directive 2002/58/EC art. 5(3), in Romania Law 506/2004 art. 4(5)-(6) (check numbering): storing or reading
  information on a terminal requires consent **unless** it is strictly necessary to provide a service explicitly requested by the user (or solely for
  the transmission of a communication). The rule covers `localStorage`, `sessionStorage` and similar, not only cookies (WP29 Opinion 04/2012 on the
  cookie exemption; EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 on the technical scope of art. 5(3)).
- **`sessionStorage` seat (room code, player id, token):** necessary to deliver the requested service (staying seated in a live room after a dropped connection); it is
  a session-authentication and session-continuity measure; it is deleted when the tab closes; it is never read by anyone else. Strictly necessary.
- **`localStorage` language:** a user-interface preference. WP29 treats user-chosen interface customisation (such as language) as exempt when it is set by the user's own action and used only for
  that purpose; it is less clear-cut for long-lived storage. Points in favour here: it is written only when the player clicks the language toggle (verified: `setLanguage` writes, `init` only reads), it contains one two-letter value, it is not an
  identifier, is never sent to the server, and is disclosed in the text. Residual risk is low; **[lawyer]** to confirm. If the owner wants zero residual risk, the alternative is to keep the choice in memory (and the browser language as default) or in `sessionStorage`.
- The text should still **inform** (art. 5(3) information duty is satisfied by the existing sentence; art. 13 does not apply to storage that contains no personal data, but the sentence is good practice). Keep it.

## Duties of the controller that the page cannot show (for the owner)

1. **Record of processing (art. 30).** The exemption in art. 30(5) (fewer than 250 employees) does not apply when the processing is not occasional; a continuously running public game is not occasional, so keep a short record. One page: purposes, categories, recipients, transfers, retention, security measures.
2. **Processor agreements (art. 28):** accept and file Cloudflare's DPA; confirm and file the static host's DPA. Note the sub-processor lists.
3. **Transfer mechanism check:** SCCs or DPF coverage for Cloudflare; the host's location. Keep a note.
4. **Rights-request procedure:** one mailbox, one-month answer time (art. 12(3)), a template reply saying that rooms are deleted automatically and what can still be found (usually nothing), the identity check rule (art. 11).
5. **Breach procedure (art. 33-34):** who decides, 72-hour notice to ANSPDCP when there is risk to people; the data is low-risk, but a seat-token leak or an unauthorised read of a room is a breach to be assessed; keep a log of breaches even if not notified. Security review reports in `docs/reviews/` are good evidence for art. 32.
6. **Legitimate-interest assessment** for the IP/rate limit (S3).
7. **Decision note on DPIA/DPO not required**, and the reasoning for "public result is not personal data".
8. **Terms of use:** if 6(1)(b) is relied on, a two-line "by playing you ask for the service described" is implied; a separate terms page is not needed.
9. **ANSPDCP:** no registration or prior notification exists any more; nothing to file.
10. **Review the text when anything changes** (new provider, analytics, voice, accounts, longer retention, a public list with free-text names).

Length: about 330 words in English; folded in the footer this is acceptable. Terms kept in English follow the game's rule ("framework", "escaped defects", "cycle time"). The footer-level mention of "Cloudflare" is a vendor name: the project's copy rule forbids vendor names in the text, but art. 13(1)(e) requires the *categories* of recipients, not names, so the generic "a network and hosting provider" is **sufficient legally**; naming is good practice and the lawyer can decide. The copy above names Cloudflare for honesty; replace by "a network provider" if the owner insists on the vendor-free rule (then the DPA list lives in the records).

Separate page and contact address: a separate privacy page is **not** unavoidable (the folded text works, provided it is reachable by one click from every screen with the footer). A **contact address is unavoidable** (R1, R4, R7).
