# Fidelity review of Two Pizzas against the Scrum Guide (2020), the Kanban Guide v2025.5 and the Agile Manifesto

Read-only review, no file changed.

**Sources and their limits.** All three sources were fetched on 2026-11-16 (the two guides from the Scrum Guide and Kanban Guide pages; the Manifesto principles page was fetched too). The fetch tool returned condensed summaries, not full text, and refused to reproduce the guides verbatim. Where I quote a guide below, the wording comes from my reading of the guide text and is **from memory, unverified verbatim** unless marked "(fetched)". Confirmed by fetch:
- Timeboxes: Planning at most 8 h, Daily 15 min, Review at most 4 h, Retro at most 3 h, Sprint one month or less.
- Purposes of the four events and the three commitments (Product Goal, Sprint Goal, Definition of Done).
- Kanban Guide: the four metrics, the Service Level Expectation, "no meetings prescribed", and "must explicitly control the number of work items in a workflow from started to finished". The guide does not use the phrase "WIP limit". It does use "pull system".

I read all of `en.ts`. I read `ro.ts` in the phase, step, tour and notes sections and spot-checked the rest for fidelity.

## 1. Wrong (contradicts a guide)

**W1. The Sprint is shown as only the work phase.**
- Keys: `phase.scrum.work.name` = "Sprint", `phase.scrum.work.purpose`, `phase.resume.scrum` ("The Sprint continues after the Daily Scrum, for its second half"). The sequence is Planning, then "Sprint", then Review and Retro as separate phases. The Daily Scrum, though, is a pause inside it.
- Guide (Scrum Guide, Scrum Events): "All the work necessary to achieve the Product Goal, including Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective, happen within Sprints." Sprints are "fixed length events of one month or less to create consistency". The Retrospective "concludes the Sprint", and a new Sprint starts immediately after.
- Effect: a beginner learns that Planning, Review and Retro sit outside the Sprint.
- Fix:
  - Rename the phase card "Sprint (the work part)".
  - Purpose: "The Sprint is the container for all the other events. Here it is the time in which Developers work on the Sprint Backlog toward the Sprint Goal."
  - Add to `.real`: "One month or less, fixed length; Planning, Daily Scrum, Review and Retrospective all happen inside it."
  - Same change in Romanian.

**W2. "Soft timebox: you may run over".**
- Keys: `phase.ends.soft` ("Soft timebox: you may run over, and the overrun is recorded."), `timer.soft`, `timer.overtime`, `a11y.overtime`.
- Guide: the event limits are maxima ("at most"); the Scrum Master ensures events are "kept within the timebox". "Soft timebox" is not a Scrum term.
- Fix: `phase.ends.soft` = "In this game the clock keeps running after the time is up, and the overrun is recorded. In Scrum an event must end within its timebox." Same change in Romanian.

**W3. The Product Owner "accepts or rejects" work.**
- Keys: `phase.scrum.work.role.productOwner` ("accepts or rejects the work at the Sprint Review"), `phase.scrum.inspect.role.productOwner` ("accepts or rejects each done item"), `inspect.verdict.accepted`, `inspect.verdict.rejected` ("Rejected: escaped defect"), `inspect.verdict.acceptedText`, `inspect.verdict.rejectedText`. The same mechanic is in spec 3.3 and 3.5.
- Guide (Artifacts, Increment): work is part of the Increment when it meets the Definition of Done, and the Scrum Team decides that. The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing value and for the Product Backlog. The Guide has no acceptance gate. The Review is where attendees "collaborate on what to do next" and the Product Backlog may be adjusted.
- Effect: "reject at Review" teaches the old waterfall-style acceptance.
- Fix: keep the mechanic but rename it as the game's own judge. Roles: "The game checks each finished item's answer. In real Scrum there is no acceptance step: work that meets the Definition of Done is the Increment, and the Product Owner and stakeholders react to it." Verdict labels: "Right answer" / "Wrong answer (escaped defect)".

**W4. The Product Owner "waits" and "does not write notes".**
- Keys: `phase.scrum.work.role.productOwner` ("waits"), `phase.scrum.retro.role.productOwner` ("does not write notes").
- Guide (Scrum Team; Sprint Retrospective): "The Scrum Team discusses…" The whole Scrum Team, including the Product Owner, takes part in the Retro. The Product Owner collaborates with the Developers during the Sprint, and scope "may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner".
- Fix: the game as Product Owner is an acceptable simplification. The copy must say so once, for example "In real Scrum a person is the Product Owner. They are available during the Sprint to clarify items and negotiate scope, and they take part in the Retrospective."

**W5. "Roles".**
- Keys: `start.framework.scrum.line` ("five events and three roles, as in the Scrum Guide"), plus the user-visible "role" in `phase.rolesLabel` ("Who does what"), `lobby.volunteer`, `phase.youAre`, `a11y.player`, and similar.
- Guide (Scrum Team): "accountabilities" — Developers, Product Owner, Scrum Master. The Guide itself does not call them roles.
- Fix: "Sprints, five events and three accountabilities, as in the Scrum Guide." Same change in Romanian ("trei responsabilități"). For the player-facing role label, either keep "role" as the game's word or use "accountability" on the cards.

**W6. Kanban start line contradicts round 1.**
- Keys: `start.framework.kanban.line` ("a limit on work in progress") against `limits.none` ("Round 1 has no limits") and `phase.kanban.work.purpose` ("while the team keeps the number of items in progress under control" — in round 1 nothing controls it).
- Guide (Kanban Guide, Practices): "Kanban system members must explicitly control the number of work items…". Controlling WIP is part of managing flow. A system without it is not Kanban by the guide.
- Fix: say that round 1 is a deliberate "before" picture. `limits.none` = "Round 1 has no WIP control on purpose, so you can feel what uncontrolled work does. Kanban itself asks you to control it." Add the same sentence to the `phase.kanban.work.practice` card. `phase.kanban.work.purpose` should read "…while the team learns to keep it under control (from round 2 the game enforces it)".

## 2. Undisclosed practice (shown as if part of the framework, no "not in the guide" note at the point of use)

Positives: the cards disclose planning poker and story points (`phase.scrum.plan.practice`), velocity (`.inspect.practice`), the Daily Scrum questions (`.daily.practice`), the retro format (`.retro.practice`), and all Kanban meetings (`phase.kanban.*.practice` and `.real`). The `inspect.velocityNote` and `limits.practice` keys disclose their practices too. Gaps:

**U1. The disclosure sits in a collapsed block with an unhelpful label.**
- `Card.tsx:52–56`: the "Good to know" notes are inside a fold whose summary reads only "In real life · Typical mistake".
- Fix: add `phase.practiceLabel` to the fold summary, or show the practice line outside the fold.

**U2. Poker has no note on its own screen.**
- Keys: `poker.title`, `poker.note`, `script.scrum.plan.estimate.*`, `poker.velocity`. The page headed "Planning poker" never says it is not in the Guide.
- Fix: `poker.note` = "Planning poker and these numbers are a common practice, not part of the Scrum Guide. The numbers are the team's own: talk first, then vote." Same change in Romanian.

**U3. Velocity is used to plan with no disclosure there.**
- Keys: `script.scrum.plan.commit.2` ("compare the sum of the estimates with the velocity of Sprint 1"), `observation.plan.overcommitted`, `observation.plan.fitsVelocity`, `poker.velocity`.
- The Guide (Sprint Planning) says only that Developers use "past performance", capacity and the Definition of Done to forecast.
- Fix: append "Velocity is a common practice, not part of the Scrum Guide." to `script.scrum.plan.commit.2`, or fold it into the observation text.

**U4. The WIP limit is offered as a Scrum action.**
- Keys: `agreement.wip_limit.*` (also in Scrum), `retro.help.p.try.wip`, `retro.help.q.overload`, `observation.work.wipLow` ("Work in progress stayed low", positive, in Scrum).
- The Scrum Guide has no WIP limits.
- Fix: one note on `agreement.wip_limit.meaning`, Scrum variant: "A WIP limit comes from Kanban; a Scrum team may adopt it as its own agreement."

**U5. The Kanban facilitator and customer roles are never marked as game inventions.**
- Keys: `role.facilitator.kanban`, `phase.kanban.*.role.facilitator`, `phase.kanban.inspect.role.customer` ("accepts or rejects each finished item").
- Guide (Kanban Guide): defines no roles or accountabilities, only "Kanban system members".
- Fix: add to the first Kanban card or to `lobby.facilitatorHint.kanban`: "The Kanban Guide defines no roles. The facilitator, the customer's verdicts and the rounds are this game's way of running a session." Also note that Kanban has no iterations (`iteration.kanban` "Round").

**U6. The Kanban "no timebox" claim contradicts the clock.**
- Key: `phase.kanban.work.real` ("There is no timebox: flow is continuous") against the hard clock in `phase.ends.hard` and `phase.kanban.work.role.facilitator` ("keeps the round inside its timebox").
- Fix: "Kanban has no timebox. This game gives each round a clock so a session fits in under an hour."

**U7. Named in the game, not in either guide, with no note:**
- Escaped defect: the label is used in both frameworks and across summaries and the leaderboard.
- Replenishment (`phase.kanban.plan.name`): the card's `.practice` and `.real` do disclose it, so this is minor.
- "Walk the board from the right" is disclosed for Scrum only. The Kanban `.practice` says only that the meeting is a common practice.
- The Kanban daily's "closest to done first" is partly covered by the guide's "avoid unnecessary aging", which it should cite.

**U8. "Also called: the demo".**
- Keys: `phase.scrum.inspect.aka`, in Romanian too. The Guide never uses "demo", and `phase.scrum.inspect.mistake` warns against treating the Review as a demo to applaud. The card contradicts itself.
- Fix: drop `.aka`, or write "Often called 'the demo' (not the Guide's word; the Review is a working session, not a presentation)". Do the same for `.daily.aka` "the daily" and `.retro.aka` "the retro" (harmless nicknames, but they are not the Guide's names either).

## 3. Outdated (2017 wording or earlier)

**O1. "Commit" in the old sense.**
- Keys: `observation.plan.overcommitted` ("More committed than last time"), `script.scrum.plan.estimate.4` ("enough to commit"), and the internal "commitment" step in spec 3.5.
- Guide (2020, Sprint Planning and Sprint Backlog): the Developers select and forecast items; the commitment of the Sprint Backlog is the Sprint Goal.
- Fix: "More work selected than last time"; "Stop estimating when you have enough to choose the Sprint's work". For `commit.*`, "Sprint Backlog" is fine as it is.

**O2. "Pull" in Scrum.**
- Keys: `step.scrum.plan.commit`, `commit.pull` ("Pull into the Sprint").
- Guide: "select". "Pull" is the Kanban and lean word.
- Fix: "Select items for the Sprint Backlog". Polish.

## 4. Misleading simplifications

**M1. Sprint Goal as "finish every item of a theme".**
- Keys: `goal.option`, `goal.optionDetail`, `inspect.goal`, `observation.inspect.goalMet` and `goalMissed`, and the +5 bonus.
- Guide (Artifacts, Sprint Backlog): the Sprint Goal is "the single objective for the Sprint", the Developers' commitment. It "provides flexibility in terms of the exact work needed". If the work turns out different than expected, the Developers negotiate scope with the Product Owner.
- Effect: the game turns the Goal into a scope checklist. That is the opposite of its purpose, and the "no re-negotiation" mechanic reinforces it.
- Verdict: an acceptable simplification only if framed. The Developers' vote is good; the Product Owner "offering" is weaker.
- Fix:
  - Reword the goal as an objective: "Make the Payments theme work end to end".
  - Add a line to `phase.scrum.plan.practice` or `goal.hint`: "A real Sprint Goal is an objective, not a list; the exact items may change during the Sprint."
  - Frame "all items" as the game's way of measuring the goal.

**M2. The Definition of Done is reduced to peer review and never explained.**
- Keys: `phase.scrum.work.purpose` (the only place "Definition of Done" appears), `tour.7.text` ("An item is done only when someone who did not work on it says LGTM"), spec 3.3.
- Guide (Artifacts, Increment): the Definition of Done is "a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product", created by the Scrum Team. The game never defines the term for a beginner, or says that it is the team's own.
- Verdict: peer review is a fine DoD for a game. But a beginner reads the DoD as "reviewed by someone else", and an LGTM on a wrong answer is the lesson, which needs one sentence.
- Fix: `phase.scrum.work.practice` = "In this game the Definition of Done is: reviewed by someone who did not work on the item. Real teams write their own; it describes what quality an Increment must have." In Kanban, call the review step "an explicit policy" (Kanban Guide, DoW).

**M3. The Scrum Master directs and decides.**
- Keys: the SM scripts (`script.scrum.plan.goal.2` "Ask everyone to vote, then confirm one goal", `script.scrum.plan.estimate.*`, `script.scrum.inspect.*`, `script.scrum.retro.*`), `poker.facilitatorOnly` (the facilitator reveals the cards and records the estimate), and the rotation of the role (`lobby.facilitatorHint.scrum`).
- Guide (Scrum Master): the SM is accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness and ensures events take place and are positive, productive and kept within the timebox. The Developers decide what to select and how. The Guide says nothing about rotation. Making the SM the one who confirms the goal or records estimates conflicts with the Developers' ownership.
- Verdict: practical for a game, and an SM with a script is acceptable. Rotation is not contrary to the Guide.
- Fix: one line on the Scrum start card: "Here the Scrum Master presses the buttons for the Developers' decisions. In real Scrum the Developers decide and the Scrum Master ensures the events happen." Keep the Daily Scrum lines (`script.scrum.daily.walk.2`), which are right.

**M4. Estimates as cards, and velocity from one four-minute Sprint.**
- Keys: spec 3.5, `poker.*`, `inspect.velocity`.
- Verdict: acceptable and disclosed (see U2, U3). Add: "Velocity from a single Sprint is a weak forecast."

**M5. The Kanban metrics are loosened.**
- Keys: `kanban.metric.wip.text` ("Highest value: {value}"), `kanban.metric.throughput.text` ("Items finished in the round"), `kanban.metric.cycleTime.text` ("Average: {value}"), `kanban.compare.*` (averages and medians).
- Guide (Kanban Guide, Flow Metrics): WIP is the number started and not finished. Throughput is the number finished per unit of time. Cycle time is per item, from start to finish. Work Item Age is the elapsed time from start to now for an open item. The Guide also asks for an SLE: a probability and an elapsed time ("85% of work items will be finished in eight days or less").
- Effect: the game teaches the averages and never mentions the SLE. The SLE is part of the Definition of Workflow (DoW).
- Fix: throughput text = "Items finished per round"; `kanban.metric.wip.text` = "Items started and not yet finished (highest in the round: {value})". Add an SLE sentence to `phase.kanban.inspect.purpose` or `.practice`: "The Kanban Guide asks a team to state a Service Level Expectation, such as '85% of items finished in X minutes', from its cycle times."

**M6. The Kanban Guide's own definitions are missing.**
- Search `en.ts` for "Definition of Workflow", "practices" and "SLE": none appear. The Kanban start line and cards never state the three practices (define and visualize the workflow, actively manage items, improve the workflow). The columns are shown as fixed (Ready, In Progress, Review, Done) with no mention that the team defines them.
- Fix: one card sentence: "Kanban asks a team to define and visualize its workflow (here: Ready, In Progress, Review, Done), actively manage the items in it, and improve it. Your team chooses its own columns in real life."

**M7. One Daily Scrum in the middle of a Sprint.**
- Keys: `daily.clockStopped`, `phase.scrum.daily.real`.
- The real event is every working day. The `.real` line is accurate. Add to `phase.scrum.daily.practice`: "In real life it happens every working day; here you have it once."

**M8. `observation.daily.planUnchanged`** ("The board did not change") implies the Daily should always change the plan. The Guide says "adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary".
- Fix: neutral wording: "No change was made. That is fine if none was needed."

**M9. The Daily Scrum "any player may end it"** and the "walk from the right" rule are game rules. The Guide does not say who may end the Daily Scrum. This is covered by `.practice` for the structure but not for "any player may end it".

## 6. Learning goal

After the phase cards and the summary sheet, a beginner can state the four events' durations and purposes correctly, because the wording follows the Guide closely.

Gaps:
1. The summary sheet (`Summary.tsx:18`, `Events = ['plan', 'daily', 'inspect', 'retro']`) omits the Sprint, the first Scrum event. Its duration ("one month or less") and its purpose are not on the sheet. Fix: add 'work' to `Events` for Scrum, with the W1 wording.
2. The summary states that "the purpose, the timebox and the typical mistake of each event" is what you learn (`summary.learned`). For Kanban there are no events and no timeboxes (the `.real` lines say so). Fix: a Kanban variant of `summary.learned`: "The Kanban Guide prescribes no events; you have tried the common meetings teams hold, and the four flow measures it names." `tour.8.text` ("each with a purpose and a timebox") has the same issue.
3. The "Good to know" disclosure is folded (U1).

## 7. Kanban specifically

- Three practices, DoW, SLE: not stated (M6).
- Four measures: all four are named and defined accurately enough, with the polish in M5.
- No meetings: stated clearly on every Kanban card (`.real`), and the meetings are marked a common practice. This is faithful. `phase.kanban.plan.purpose`, `.daily.purpose` and `.inspect.purpose` present their meetings as the game's way.
- Beyond the guide as if it were the guide: the single-number WIP limit per column. `limits.practice`, `limits.explain` and the `phase.kanban.plan.practice` card do disclose it. The agreement "Set WIP limits" and the "suggested after round 1" script (`script.kanban.retro.action.2`) are the game's design.
- "Pull system": the Guide does say controlling WIP "should create a pull system" (fetched). The game enforces limits but never names it. That is fine, not wrong.
- Fixed columns: acceptable if the team is told it normally defines them (M6).

## Score: 7/10

The wording of the event cards, durations and disclosures is close to the sources. What lowers the score is the model of the framework: the Sprint shown as the work phase only, acceptance by the Product Owner, soft timeboxes, a Sprint Goal that is a checklist, and a Kanban part that omits the Guide's own definitions.

The changes that would raise it most:
1. W1 and W2: restore the Sprint as the container, and say events must end inside their timebox.
2. W3 and W4: reword the Product Owner's accept/reject and absence, and say once that a person plays the Product Owner in real Scrum.
3. M1 and M2: reframe the Sprint Goal as an objective, and define the Definition of Done in the game's own words.
4. M6: add the Kanban three practices, the DoW and the SLE in one card.
5. U1 to U4: move the "not in the Guide" notes out of the fold and onto the poker screen, the velocity line and the WIP agreement.
6. The learning-goal fixes: add the Sprint to the summary sheet and change the Kanban "events" wording.
